31. Key ideas: Speaker cone vibrates → creates compression waves (areas of dense and spread particles) → these compressions travel through air (the medium) → reach eardrum → eardrum vibrates → brain interprets as sound.

32. Key ideas: Morse Code is DIGITAL — a coded pattern of two distinct signals (dots/dashes); the telephone is ANALOG — it converts continuous sound wave vibrations into electrical signals that carry the actual shape of the sound wave, allowing voice to be transmitted.

33. Key ideas: Light slows down when it enters a denser material (like water). When a wave slows down, it bends. The amount of bending depends on the angle of entry and the difference in density between the two materials.

34. Key ideas: Pupil/Iris — controls the amount of light entering. Lens — focuses (refracts) the light rays onto the retina. Retina — contains light-sensitive cells (rods and cones) that detect the light and send signals to the brain via the optic nerve. Accept any two of these with accurate descriptions.

PART 5: HISTORY & DISCOVERY (Rubric: 5 pts each)
Award points for:
  • Historical accuracy (2 pts)
  • Scientific reasoning (2 pts)
  • Clear explanation (1 pt)

35. "E" appears most in English text. Shorter codes = fewer signals needed = faster total transmission. This is exactly like data compression today — encode frequent items with shorter codes, rare items with longer codes.

36. All three (fire, drums, mirrors) use WAVES to carry encoded information across distances — drums use sound waves, fire/mirrors use light waves. The underlying principle is the same: encode a message as a pattern in wave signals and transmit across a medium a sender cannot cross physically.

37. Both systems use exactly TWO states: Morse (dot/dash), Binary (0/1). Any message can be fully encoded using just two distinct options combined in different sequences. This is the foundational concept of digital communication.

38. Light travels faster than electricity through copper. Light waves also experience less signal loss over long distances. Fiber optic cables can carry vastly more data simultaneously (higher frequency/bandwidth) than copper wire. Combined, these properties make light the superior medium for long-distance, high-speed internet communication.

39. Use the mirror to reflect the flashlight beam toward the helicopter — this is REFLECTION. To increase amplitude (brightness), angle the mirror so it focuses the flashlight beam directly at the helicopter rather than scattering it. Flash a recognizable pattern (e.g., SOS = 3 short, 3 long, 3 short flashes).

40. A hearing aid detects incoming sound waves and uses a microphone + amplifier to increase the AMPLITUDE of the waves before sending them into the ear canal. Larger amplitude = the eardrum is displaced more = the person perceives the sound as louder.

41. This is called Signal INTERFERENCE — two waves of the same frequency overlap and disrupt each other. Engineers solve this by switching the router to a different channel (frequency) that is not used by the neighboring router, so signals no longer interfere.

42. Fiber optic (light signals) recommendation — two of:
    • Light travels faster than electrical signals through copper.
    • Light waves experience less amplitude loss (signal degradation) over long distances.
    • Fiber optic cables can carry much more data at the same time (higher frequencies/bandwidth).
    • Light signals are not affected by electromagnetic interference from nearby power sources.
