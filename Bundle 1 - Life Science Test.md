PART 8: READING COMPREHENSION (12 points)

Read the passage below and answer the questions.

The Amazing Arctic Fox

The Arctic fox is a master of survival in one of Earth's harshest environments. Living in the frozen tundra where temperatures can drop to -50°F, this small animal has developed remarkable adaptations.

In winter, the Arctic fox grows a thick white coat. This coat serves two important purposes: the white color provides camouflage against the snow, helping it hide from predators and sneak up on prey. The thickness of the fur traps air, creating insulation that keeps the fox warm even in extreme cold. The fox's compact body and short legs also reduce heat loss.

The Arctic fox has an interesting relationship with food. In summer, when prey like lemmings and birds are abundant, the fox hunts actively and stores extra food in underground caches. In winter, when food is scarce, it digs up these hidden food stores. The Arctic fox will even follow polar bears and eat the leftover scraps from their kills.

The fox's feet are covered with thick fur, even on the bottom. This prevents frostbite and provides traction on ice. Its keen sense of hearing allows it to locate prey moving under the snow. When it hears a lemming tunneling below, the fox leaps high in the air and pounces down through the snow to catch its meal.

As summer arrives, the Arctic fox sheds its white coat and grows a brown or gray one, blending in with the rocky, snow-free tundra. This seasonal color change helps it stay camouflaged year-round.
